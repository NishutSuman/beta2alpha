## Core Philosophy
The market is **fractal** — the same institutional delivery algorithm repeats across every
timeframe. **HTF defines the narrative; LTF provides execution precision.**

The objective is not to predict every move, but to identify:
1. The institutional narrative
2. The intended liquidity destination
3. The path price is likely to follow
4. The highest-probability execution point

**Delivery model:** Liquidity → Mitigation → Displacement → Consolidation → Continuation → Liquidity Delivery

## Core Principles
- Trade with the HTF narrative.
- Every trade must have a defined liquidity destination.
- Institutions move price from one liquidity pool to another.
- LTFs refine execution, not bias. Bias alone is never an entry.
- If the narrative becomes invalid, restart the analysis.
- Preserve capital over forcing trades.

## Step 1 — Map External Liquidity
- **Weekly:** PWH = Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL); PWL = Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL)
- **Daily:** PDH = BSL; PDL = SSL
- **Liquidity hierarchy (priority):** 1) Prev Week H/L, 2) Prev Day H/L, 3) Major Swing H/L, 4) Session H/L, 5) Internal liquidity. Higher priority = greater institutional significance.

## Step 2 — Determine HTF Bias
Never search for entries before determining the narrative. Determine whether price is reacting from a Daily Order Block / FVG / Breaker / Mitigation block. Ask:
- Is price respecting or rejecting the PD Array?
- Is displacement supporting the reaction?
- Does market structure agree?
- Is the reaction supported by liquidity?

Only set bias when **multiple factors agree**. Never on one signal.

## Step 3 — Identify the Market Destination
Every move needs a destination. Determine: which liquidity pool price is seeking, which HTF OB/FVG may terminate the move, internal vs external liquidity. This defines direction, target, and expected reversal area. **Never look for entries until the destination is known.**

## Step 4 — Build the Intraday Narrative (Daily → 4H → 1H → 15M → 5M)
Every timeframe should support the same story.
- **Bullish:** SSL sweep → Bullish MSS → Bullish OB → Bullish FVG → Bullish displacement
- **Bearish:** BSL sweep → Bearish MSS → Bearish OB → Bearish FVG → Bearish displacement

Temporary pullbacks are fine if they realign. Never let a 5M chart override the Daily narrative.

## Step 5 — Validate the Order Block (only high-quality PD Arrays)
- **Rule 1 — Liquidity First:** Liquidity Sweep → Order Block → Displacement → Fair Value Gap
- **Rule 2 — Extreme OB:** Bullish → lowest bullish OB; Bearish → highest bearish OB
- **Rule 3 — Institutional confirmation:** the OB should engineer liquidity, produce displacement, leave imbalance, respect HTF bias. More confirmations = higher probability.
- **Rule 4 — FVG:** strengthens an OB but isn't absolute; a valid OB can exist without a textbook FVG if HTF confluence + strong displacement + intact narrative.
- **Rule 5 — Inside Bar exception:** if an inside bar creates the OB, extend the zone to include imbalance; valid only with institutional confirmation, else ignore.

## Step 6 — Multi-Timeframe Fractal Alignment
Daily → 4H → 1H → 15M → 5M → Execution. Every TF should confirm: Liquidity Sweep, MSS, Order Block, FVG, Displacement, Liquidity Target. LTFs refine; never replace HTF.

## Narrative Invalidation (discard immediately, restart from Step 1)
- Daily MSS changes direction
- HTF OB decisively violated
- Liquidity target reached before entry
- Major news flips sentiment
- LTFs continuously fail to align

## Golden Rules
Never predict—follow delivery · Never trade without a destination · HTF always wins · LTF only precision · Don't force an OB · Don't force an FVG · Every trade needs objective confluence · Protect capital before profit.

## Step 7 — ILDRE (how price reaches the destination)
Institutions engineer liquidity, mitigate, trap, then continue. ILDRE = understand the delivery path, don't chase.
- **7.1 Intermediate rejection zones:** HTF OBs, FVGs, breaker/mitigation blocks become pullback / temporary-rejection / mitigation areas before the final target.
- **7.2 Liquidity engineering:** create → sweep → mitigate → induce retail → resume trend. Never assume the first pullback is a reversal. Ask: is this changing the narrative or just engineering liquidity?
- **7.3 Continuation structure:** after rejection, price often consolidates; liquidity builds both sides; find the next supporting OB aligned with HTF = Continuation Zone.
- **7.4 Liquidity hunt before continuation:** Consolidation → Liquidity build-up → Sweep → Mitigation of supporting PD array → Continuation. **Wait for the sweep first.**
- **7.5 Behaviour at continuation zone:** (1) immediate consolidation, (2) small displacement then consolidation, (3) immediate continuation (rare, strong momentum). Markets are probabilistic.
- **7.6 Confirmation:** consolidation breakout + full 15M close + breakout in HTF direction + no immediate rejection back inside.

## Step 8 — Entry Refinement
After 15M confirmation, drop to 5M and find: extreme OB, FVG, MSS, supporting liquidity. If all align → execution zone.
- **8.1 Fallback:** if no valid 5M PD array, return to 15M extreme OB + FVG + structure.
- **Execution priority:** 1) Extreme 5M OB + FVG, 2) Valid 15M OB + FVG, 3) No valid zone → **No Trade.** Bias is never an entry.

## Step 9 — Trade Execution
- **Entry:** only from a validated PD Array, in HTF direction, confirmed institutional continuation.
- **Stop Loss:** beyond the extreme OB / liquidity sweep / structural invalidation — the point where the idea is wrong. Never random.
- **Take Profit (priority):** 1) HTF liquidity target, 2) next HTF rejection zone, 3) swing H/L, 4) PDH/PDL, 5) PWH/PWL. Partials allowed.

## Risk Management
Never exceed max daily loss · never increase risk after a loss · no revenge trading · never move SL further · never widen risk to "save" a trade · never enter late after displacement. Capital preservation first.

## Market Condition Filter (best in directional markets — avoid when)
HTF bias unclear · price in a large range · no clear liquidity destination · major news imminent · LTFs constantly contradict HTF. **No setup is better than a poor setup.**

## Fundamental & Geopolitical Confluence
Rate decisions, CPI, PPI, NFP, FOMC, central-bank speeches, geopolitics, risk-on/off, currency strength. Fundamentals add confidence; they don't replace technicals.
